# Play Store listing for Kharcha

Copy these into the Google Play Console. The pictures are in this folder.

## Main store listing

**App name** (30 characters or fewer)

    Kharcha: Daily Expense Tracker

**Short description** (80 characters or fewer)

    Log spends, set budget limits and never forget money you lent.

**Full description** (4000 characters or fewer)

    Kharcha is a simple daily spend book. Write down what you spend, see where the money went, and stay inside a budget that fits your salary.

    What you can do
    • Log a spend in a few taps. Pick a category, how you paid (UPI, cash or card), and mark it as a need, a want, or something you could skip.
    • See each day at a glance: how much you spent, how that compares with your daily limit, and how much of it could have been saved.
    • Set a budget from your monthly salary and the amount you want to save. Kharcha works out what you can spend each month, each day and in each category.
    • Set your own daily, monthly and category limits. You get an alert when you reach 80% of a limit and again when you cross it.
    • Record money you lent to friends and family. Add the date you gave it and when it should come back, note part payments, and get reminders so you do not forget to ask for it back.
    • Get advice on where money is leaking, such as food delivery, small spends that add up, subscriptions and expensive weekends.

    Automatic recording (optional)
    If you allow notification access, Kharcha can read payment notifications and bank messages on your phone and add the spends for you. It keeps only messages about money you spent. Kharcha does not upload, share or sell your information. You can turn this off at any time in Android settings. Cash spends still need to be added by hand.

    Private by design
    No account, no ads and no tracking. Your spends, budget and loans are stored on your phone. If Google backup is on, Android keeps a backup in your own Google account, so your data comes back on a new phone. You can also save a backup file.

    Kharcha is a tracking tool. It does not move money, give loans or connect to your bank account.

**App category:** Finance

**Tags:** Expense tracker, Budgeting, Personal finance

## App content answers

These are the answers that match how the app works. Read each question in the Console and check it still fits, because the forms change.

| Section | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Privacy policy | Add the URL above. The policy is also reachable inside the app from the footer of the screens. |
| Ads | The app contains no ads. |
| App access | All features work without signing in. No login details are needed for review. |
| Target audience | Adults only (18 and over). Not designed for children. |
| Content rating | Fill the questionnaire honestly. The app has no violence, no user generated content and no online interaction. Expect a low rating. |
| Financial features | Kharcha only tracks the user's own spending and money they lent to friends. It is not a bank, lender, payment app or loan service. Choose the answer that says the app has no financial services features, or the closest personal finance or budgeting option the form offers. |
| Data safety | The developer runs no server and receives no user data. Android Auto Backup may copy the app data to the user's own Google account, a service run by the operating system. Google's guidance on the Data safety form says data backed up only through Android Auto Backup does not need to be declared as collected, but read the current wording of the form and decide. Kharcha does not send any user data to the developer or third parties. Spends, budgets, loans and the text of payment notifications are handled only on the phone, and notification text is thrown away after the amount and merchant are saved. Google defines "collected" as sent off the device, so the usual answer is that no data is collected or shared. Check this against the current wording of the form. |
| Notification access | Google asks for a clear in-app explanation before a sensitive permission. The app shows one (the "Before you turn this on" screen) with Agree and No thanks buttons. Mention the feature in the store description, as above. Reviewers may ask how the feature works, so keep a short screen recording of turning it on ready. |

## Before you can publish

1. A Google Play developer account (one time registration fee, and Google checks your identity).
2. For a personal account created after 13 November 2023, Google requires a closed test with at least 12 testers who stay opted in for 14 days before you can apply for production access. Start collecting testers early. Ask friends and classmates to join with their Gmail addresses.
3. New apps and updates must target Android 16 (API 36) from 31 August 2026. The native build in this project uses Capacitor 8, which targets API 36.
