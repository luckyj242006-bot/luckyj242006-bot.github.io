Kharcha is a daily spend book for Android and the web. It helps you log what you spend, keep to a budget, and remember money you lent to other people. This page explains what the app does with your information. The short version: your information stays on your phone, and the Android app can keep a backup in your own Google account.

What Kharcha stores

Kharcha stores the things you enter or that you allow it to add:

  • Your spends: amount, date, category, a note, and how you paid.
  • Your salary, savings goal and spending limits.
  • Loans you record: the name you type, the amount, the dates and any note.

All of this is saved in the app's private storage on your device. There is no account and no sign-in. The information is not sent to the developer or to anyone else, so the developer cannot see it.

Backup

In the Android app, Android's own backup service can save Kharcha's data (your spends, budget, limits and loans) to your own Google account, in the private backup space Google provides for apps. This is done by Android and Google, not by the developer. The developer has no server, no access to your backup and cannot read it. The backup is used only to bring your data back when you reinstall Kharcha or set up a new phone with the same Google account. It follows Google's own privacy policy and uses your Google storage allowance. You can turn it off in your phone settings under Google, then Backup, or delete it there. Kharcha's private queue of payment notifications is left out of the backup.

You can also save a backup file from the Backup panel in the app. The file goes wherever you choose to send it, and only you control it. The web version does not back up automatically.

Notification access (optional, Android app only)

If you choose to turn on automatic recording, Android asks you to give Kharcha notification access. After you agree to this in the app and in Android settings, Kharcha reads notifications on your phone from payment apps, bank apps and message apps.

  • Kharcha keeps a notification only if it looks like money you spent, for example a UPI payment or a bank debit message. All other notifications are ignored.
  • A kept message waits in a private queue on your phone until you open Kharcha. Kharcha then turns it into a spend entry. It saves the amount, the name of who you paid, a guessed category and the payment type. The rest of the message is discarded.
  • Kharcha does not send anything from your notifications anywhere. Only the spend entries you end up with are part of your data, so they are covered by the backup described above if you have Google backup on.

You can turn this off at any time in Android settings under notification access. Kharcha works without it.

Reminders

Loan reminders and limit alerts are notifications that Kharcha creates on your own phone. They do not use a server.

What Kharcha does not do

  • It has no advertising, no analytics and no tracking.
  • It does not sell or share your information.
  • It does not load fonts or code from other companies. The fonts are part of the app.

If you open the web version in a browser, the website host receives the usual technical details of a visit, such as your IP address and the time, like any website does. The web version is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub's privacy statement covers that connection.

Deleting your information

On Android, open Settings, then Apps, then Kharcha, then Storage, and choose Clear storage. Uninstalling the app also removes everything. In a browser, clear the site data for this website. Because the developer holds none of your information, there is nothing to delete anywhere else.

Children

Kharcha is not made for children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.
